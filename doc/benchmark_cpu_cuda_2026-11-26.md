# MUSIC CPU and CUDA benchmark · November 26, 2026

The current `gpu` branch completed **220 timed runs across 34 workloads** on an Intel Core i7-9750H and NVIDIA GTX 1650. Against the faster multithreaded CPU setting per case, CUDA FP32 was **1.86–4.98×** faster for lumpy TATB and **1.81–8.49×** faster for string-source evolution. CUDA FP64 speedups ranged from **0.64–1.79×**.

## Setup

- **Revision and hardware.** MUSIC `gpu` at `48c10815c602742809d89359383d8be1ed66b33e`, measured November 26, 2026. Intel Core i7-9750H with 6 physical cores and 12 hardware threads. NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1650 with 4 GiB. All runs used one Release executable built with GCC 15.2, OpenMP, GSL 2.8 and CUDA 13.5.37 for `sm_75`.
- **Backends.** CPU used `gpu_backend 0` with 1, 6 or 12 OpenMP threads. CUDA used `gpu_backend 3`, with `gpu_float 0` for FP64 and `gpu_float 1` for FP32, and 12 host threads. OpenMP used `OMP_PLACES=cores`, `OMP_PROC_BIND=spread` and `OMP_DYNAMIC=FALSE`. The CUDA block size and `gpu_store_q` used their defaults. The latter carries conservative variables on the device in FP32. Kernel profiling was off. CUDA used userspace libraries extracted from the cached NVIDIA 620.70.06 package to match the loaded kernel module. The override applied to the benchmark process.
- **Evolution.** Three spatial dimensions, second-order Runge–Kutta, fixed `Delta_Tau 0.02` fm/c, `beastMode 0`, `face_reconstruction 0`, and net-baryon propagation. Electric-charge and strangeness propagation flags stayed at their default zero. Hydro-only mode included the standard diagnostics. Freeze-out, regridding and full evolution output were disabled.
- **Lumpy TATB.** `Initial_profile 111` at 19.6 GeV, on a 65 × 65 × 25 grid spanning 16 × 16 fm transversely and 10 units of spacetime rapidity. Each thickness function sums 40 normalized transverse Gaussians. The centers follow the integration-suite fixture with seed 7, a 2.2 fm sampling width and opposing 1 fm shifts in x. Only the individual Gaussian width σ varies. The baseline width is 0.50 fm for all four EoSs. EoS 9 also uses 0.35 and 0.80 fm. The 31 diagnostic records span τ = 1.34763–1.94763 fm/c.
- **String source terms.** `Initial_profile 13`, at a nominal 200 GeV, with 24 strings generated by the integration suite's `_write_strings_event` at seed 11 from the checked-in string fixture. The fixed grid is 49 × 49 × 33, spanning 12 × 12 fm transversely and 12 units of spacetime rapidity. Source smearing uses a transverse width of 0.50 fm and a spacetime-rapidity width of 0.50. MUSIC resolved deposition to τ = 0.50168–1.99789 fm/c, and the 91 diagnostic records span τ = 0.48–2.28 fm/c. The CUDA logs confirm on-device evaluation of the string source.
- **Transport.** The four settings add terms cumulatively. Shear uses η/s = 0.12 and second-order terms. Bulk adds the default temperature-dependent ζ/s (type 1). Diffusion adds net-baryon diffusion with `kappa_coefficient 0.2`. EoS 9 treats μ_B as zero, so enabling diffusion in that row benchmarks the enabled code path with zero μ_B/T driving gradients. EoS IDs 0, 9, 14 and 20 are the ideal gas, hotQCD (UrQMD), NEoS-BQS and NEoS-4D, respectively.
- **Timing.** Wall time runs from process launch through exit and includes loading tables, initialization, evolution, diagnostics and CUDA transfers. Runs were sequential after one CUDA warm-up. The first pass rotated backend order by workload. The selected second-pass workloads ran in reverse workload and backend order. The tables use the median of two runs where repeated and the measured time where sampled once. CUDA speedups divide the faster of the 6- and 12-thread CPU times by the relevant CUDA time.

### Timing observations

- The 12-thread CPU was faster than the 6-thread CPU in 34 of 34 workloads. It was 4.09–5.84× faster than one thread.
- FP64 beat the faster multithreaded CPU in 29 of 34 workloads. Ideal EoS 9 with string sources gave the highest FP32 speedup.
- At σ = 0.50 fm, the shear + bulk + diffusion workloads took 1.66–2.22× the ideal wall time with 12 CPU threads, and 1.25–2.87× the ideal time in CUDA FP32.
- The 10 repeated workloads had median timing spreads of 1.6%, 3.5% and 3.6% for 1, 6 and 12 CPU threads, and 4.6% and 4.8% for CUDA FP64 and FP32. Spread is the larger time divided by the smaller time minus one. The largest observed spread was 55.9% on CUDA FP32 (shear + bulk + diffusion, EoS 9, TATB at σ = 0.80 fm).

## Numerical agreement

All timed runs reached the planned final step with finite values in all eight diagnostics columns. CPU conservation histories were byte-identical across thread counts in 34 of 34 workloads. Repeat histories were byte-identical in 50 of 50 workload–backend pairs.

Each error below is the largest absolute CPU–CUDA difference over the recorded proper times, workloads and repeats in that group, divided by the peak absolute value of the same quantity from the corresponding 12-thread CPU history. The string cases start with essentially empty fluid, so the normalization uses the peak over the full history.

The largest FP32 energy-history difference was **0.351%** (shear + bulk, EoS 14, string sources). These comparisons cover integrated energy and net baryon number for the benchmark events.
